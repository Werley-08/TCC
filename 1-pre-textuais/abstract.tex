Modularity is an important attribute of software structural quality, as it is related to how responsibilities are distributed, 
dependencies are established, and components can be understood, modified, and evolved. In object-oriented systems, architectural 
decisions may directly influence characteristics such as coupling, cohesion, and structural complexity. In this context, architectural 
styles such as layered architecture and hexagonal architecture propose different ways of organizing system elements, which may produce 
distinct effects on software modularity.

Therefore, this work aims to evaluate the modularity of object-oriented systems through the application of structural metrics across 
different architectural styles. To achieve this, a comparative study will be conducted involving two systems developed in 
an academic context: SISO, structured according to layered architecture, and Roomie, structured according to hexagonal architecture. 
The analysis will be based on metrics extracted through static analysis of the source code, considering indicators related to coupling, 
cohesion, and structural complexity.

This work is expected to contribute to the understanding of how different architectural decisions may be reflected in measurable 
structural characteristics of object-oriented systems. Furthermore, it aims to provide quantitative evidence to support modularity 
analysis and assist the adoption of more grounded architectural practices in Software Engineering.
